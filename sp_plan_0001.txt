## Architecture Sketch

### System Overview

The project consists of four tightly integrated layers:

1. Knowledge Layer
   - Peer-reviewed papers
   - Authoritative industry sources
   - Course-provided technical documentation

2. Content Layer
   - Research paper chapters
   - Textbook modules (aligned with course structure)
   - Embedded citations (APA)

3. Intelligence Layer
   - AI writing agent (constitution-bound)
   - Fact-checking agent
   - Citation validation agent
   - RAG chatbot (book-grounded only)

4. Validation Layer
   - Plagiarism detection
   - Citation traceability checks
   - Readability analysis
   - RAG answer verification

---

## Section Structure

### High-Level Structure

1. Abstract
2. Introduction & Motivation
3. Background & Related Work
4. Foundations of Physical AI
5. Systems Architecture (ROS 2, Simulation, Isaac)
6. Experimental / Applied Analysis
7. Case Studies & Capstone Design
8. Discussion & Synthesis
9. Limitations & Future Work
10. Conclusion
11. References (APA)

---

## Research Approach

### Research-Concurrent Writing Model

Research and writing proceed in parallel:

- Sources are discovered and evaluated while drafting sections
- Claims are written only after source verification
- Each paragraph is traceable to at least one citation

This avoids:
- Front-loaded research delays
- Uncited or speculative writing

---

## Phase-Based Execution

### Phase 1: Research
- Identify peer-reviewed and authoritative sources
- Classify sources by relevance and credibility
- Maintain a live source registry

### Phase 2: Foundation
- Define terminology and theoretical grounding
- Establish consistent technical vocabulary
- Align with course learning outcomes

### Phase 3: Analysis
- Examine systems, architectures, and workflows
- Compare tools, platforms, and design choices
- Present empirical or literature-backed insights

### Phase 4: Synthesis
- Integrate findings across sections
- Identify patterns, tradeoffs, and implications
- Translate insights into actionable guidance

## Decisions Needing Documentation

All major decisions must be explicitly documented with:

- Decision statement
- Available options
- Selected option
- Tradeoffs and rationale

### Key Decisions Include:

1. Simulation-first vs. hardware-first learning
   - Option A: Simulation-first (lower cost, scalable)
   - Option B: Hardware-first (higher realism, higher cost)

2. ROS 2 + Isaac vs. alternative robotics stacks
   - Tradeoff: Ecosystem maturity vs. learning curve

3. RAG chatbot grounding scope
   - Book-only grounding vs. external source access

4. Cloud-native lab vs. on-premise lab
   - CapEx vs. OpEx tradeoffs

---

## Quality Validation

### Validation Dimensions

1. Accuracy
   - All claims linked to verifiable sources

2. Reproducibility
   - Steps and configurations clearly documented

3. Citation Integrity
   - APA compliance
   - No missing or fabricated references

4. Readability
   - Flesch-Kincaid Grade 10–12

5. Originality
   - 0% plagiarism tolerance

---

## Testing Strategy

### Acceptance-Criteria–Driven Validation

Each section must pass:

1. Fact Verification Test
   - Every factual claim has a citation

2. Citation Audit
   - ≥ 50% peer-reviewed sources

3. Plagiarism Scan
   - Zero copied or improperly paraphrased text

4. Readability Test
   - Target grade level met

5. RAG Response Test
   - Chatbot answers match source text exactly
   - Refusal when data is unavailable

---

## Completion Criteria

The plan is complete when:

- All phases executed and documented
- All decisions logged with tradeoffs
- All validation tests passed
- Content is publication-ready and deployable
